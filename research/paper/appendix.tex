\section{Dataset composition and task groups}
\label{app:details}

The evaluated traces come from the public \href{https://github.com/lyptus-research/cyber-task-horizons-data}{Cyber Task Horizons} release, distributed under CC BY 4.0. The subset contains three benchmark suites with overlapping Human and Agent task records. A task group is the pair \texttt{benchmark/task\_id}; identically named tasks from different benchmarks are kept separate.

Human traces are recovered from recorded terminal input, and Agent traces from structured \texttt{bash} tool calls. These interfaces are not a shared telemetry schema. The release's Human and Agent labels describe record sources; they do not independently establish who made each decision.

\section{Trace preprocessing and score construction}

Command vectors are built from allowlisted command names, with the TF--IDF vocabulary and weights fitted on outer-fold training traces only. Artifact signatures retain distinctive artifact-name tokens while removing generic tool and path terms. For a token $t$, its training-fold weight is
\[
w(t)=\log\!\left(\frac{N+1}{\operatorname{df}(t)+1}\right)+1,
\]
where $N$ is the number of training traces and $\operatorname{df}(t)$ is the number of training signatures containing $t$. For a trace pair $(x,y)$, artifact containment is the weighted overlap divided by the smaller weighted signature mass:
\[
C_{\mathrm{IDF}}(x,y)=
\frac{\sum_t \min(c_x(t),c_y(t))w(t)}
{\min\!\left(\sum_t c_x(t)w(t),\,\sum_t c_y(t)w(t)\right)}.
\]
Here $c_x(t)$ and $c_y(t)$ are token counts in the two artifact signatures. If either signature is empty, containment is defined as zero. Unseen test-time tokens receive the maximum weight from the training fold. Equation~\ref{eq:pair-score} combines this component with command TF--IDF similarity. Prompts, task text, answers, model names, raw outputs, and timestamps are not scoring features.

\section{Metrics and uncertainty}

The false-split rate is the fraction of same-task pairs rejected by the threshold; the false-merge rate is the fraction of different-task pairs accepted. Balanced accuracy averages pair-level sensitivity and specificity. Hit@1 records whether a query's highest-ranked candidate belongs to the same benchmark-qualified task; exact ties at the top receive fractional credit.

For each metric, task-level values are averaged within each benchmark, then the three benchmark means receive equal weight. The 95\% intervals use 5,000 bootstrap draws, sampling task groups with replacement within benchmark. Paired intervals resample the same task groups for both methods before computing their difference. Intervals are conditional on the fixed outer folds and are not an independent replication.
