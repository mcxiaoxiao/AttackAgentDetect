\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Data and protocol}

The evaluation uses 97 traces from 40 benchmark-qualified task groups across InterCode-CTF, CyBench, and NYU CTF Bench: 57 Human terminal records and 40 Agent tool-call records. It includes 1,558 query--candidate pairs, comprising 148 same-task and 1,410 different-task pairs. A positive label means that both records come from the same benchmark task; it is a retrieval label, not evidence of a shared actor or activity.

All records from a benchmark--task group remain in the same outer fold. The three-fold task-held-out evaluation fits feature weights and selects thresholds on training tasks only. Metrics are first averaged within task groups and then macro-averaged across the three benchmarks. Paired 95\% confidence intervals use 5,000 benchmark-stratified task-cluster bootstrap draws. Appendix~\ref{app:details} gives the source composition and metric definitions.

\subsection{Results}

The command-and-artifact score improves the point estimates for both retrieval and pair classification over command TF--IDF alone. The false-merge rate decreases by 2.20 percentage points, while balanced accuracy increases by 4.71 points and Hit@1 by 49.71 points.

The command-and-artifact policy accepted all 148 observed same-task pairs, with no missed-link event in the 40 positive task groups. Under an independent-task-cluster sensitivity assumption, 0 of 40 groups gives a one-sided exact 95\% upper bound of 7.2\% for the share of groups with any missed link. This bound is not a confidence interval for the task-macro false-split rate.

\subsection{Scope}

The false-merge rate remains high: most different-task pairs are still linked. Human traces come from terminal captures and Agent traces from tool-call records, so execution mode is confounded with collection interface. The task groups were used during method development; these estimates are exploratory rather than independent confirmation. The data contain no Script traces or independently verified actor, campaign, or attack labels. The results therefore do not support operational attribution.
