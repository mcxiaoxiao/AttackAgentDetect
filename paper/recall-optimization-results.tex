\section{Nested experiments to reduce missed associations}
\label{sec:recall-optimization}

We ran four fixed, nested task-held-out experiment rounds on the same 177 public traces, 40 benchmark-qualified task groups, and three CTF benchmarks. The 20\% training false-merge target remained fixed. Each outer fold held out complete tasks; all threshold and candidate selection used training-fold tasks. Intervals are paired benchmark-stratified task-cluster bootstrap intervals with 5,000 draws. The cohort had already been inspected during development, so these results are exploratory rather than independent confirmation.

We assessed ten metrics: overall false-split rate (FSR), false-merge rate (FMR), Human--Agent (H--A) FSR/FMR, balanced accuracy, query coverage, Hit@1, MRR, Recall@5, and NDCG@5. Lower error rates and higher values for the remaining metrics are preferred. Changes below are percentage points relative to the behavior-only baseline.

The closest threshold-only compromise was to calibrate separate thresholds for H--A and same-mode pairs. It reduced three error-rate point estimates and increased balanced accuracy, but increased H--A FMR by 0.65 points (95\% interval $[+0.33,+1.04]$). Overall FSR and FMR intervals crossed zero. Because the method uses known pair type, its thresholds are not directly deployable when execution-mode labels are unavailable.

The third round searched a prespecified global and mode-stratified threshold grid and selected only candidates that did not regress any of the ten training-fold metrics. A new method was selected in one of three outer folds; the other two used the baseline. On held-out tasks, overall FMR increased by 1.20 points and H--A FMR by 0.78 points, with intervals above zero. All four ranking point estimates declined slightly, though their intervals crossed zero. The inner-fold Pareto candidates did not generalize to the outer folds.

The fourth round fused query-normalized percentiles from the behavior score and command-cosine score. It selected a 50:50 blend in one outer fold and fell back to baseline in the other two. Held-out FSR improved by 1.05 points and H--A FMR by 0.20 points, while overall FMR worsened by 0.64 points. Balanced accuracy rose by 0.21 points with an interval crossing zero; ranking changes were close to zero and all intervals crossed zero. This candidate therefore does not improve all ten metrics, despite improving several error-rate estimates without using execution-mode labels for scoring.

In the first round, sequence alignment lowered overall FSR by 3.38 points and H--A FSR by 4.60 points, while increasing overall FMR by 3.21 points and H--A FMR by 6.32 points; all four ranking point estimates increased. Multiscale features were near neutral. Hard-negative training and 24-dimensional profile fusion substantially increased missed links. No tested method improved all ten metrics.

Error analysis suggests two unresolved representation problems. The median shorter-to-longer trace-length ratio was 0.40 among positive pairs missed by both the baseline and the 24D-profile ranker, compared with 0.75 among positive pairs found by both. Separately, 12 groups of cross-task traces shared an identical normalized command sequence; the largest group contained 18 traces from 13 tasks. These observations motivate partial-coverage matching and evidence beyond generic command names, but do not establish that either feature will improve generalization.

The available traces also confound execution mode with collection interface: 300 Python tool calls appear in 70 Agent traces and none are represented on the Human side. These fields are excluded from shared behavior features. Same-task labels denote a CTF retrieval proxy, not a common operator, attacker, or organization. A confirmatory test requires new task groups with Human, Agent, and Script behavior collected through comparable telemetry and frozen evaluation splits.
